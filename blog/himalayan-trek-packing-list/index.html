Himalayan trek packing list: winter and summer

A packing list for a Himalayan trek in India: winter and summer lists, how to layer, what to rent or buy, and what to leave at home, with rental prices.

The 30-second answer
  • Pack in three layers (thermal base, fleece, shell) plus a warm jacket for camp. Leave jeans and cotton at home.
  • Rent the bulky, rarely used items: padded jacket, rucksack, shoes. Buy what touches your skin: thermals, socks, gloves.
  • Winter adds a -10 C jacket, microspikes and gaiters; summer and monsoon add rain gear and a pack liner.

A good Himalayan packing list is mostly about warmth and dryness: three layers, a warm jacket for camp, waterproof shoes and a head torch. The rest depends on the season, and the heaviest items, like a padded jacket and rucksack, are often cheaper to rent than to buy.

How should you layer?

REI's layering guide describes three layers. The base layer sits next to your skin and moves sweat away; synthetics (polyester, nylon) and merino wool or silk work. The insulating middle layer, such as fleece, holds in body heat. The outer shell blocks wind and rain and must be breathable enough not to trap sweat. REI also advises carrying all three on every outing, since you can shed layers but cannot add ones you left behind. Cotton holds moisture and dries slowly, which is why operators say to avoid it.

The National Park Service's Ten Essentials list adds a few non-negotiables for any hike: navigation, sun protection, extra clothing for the worst weather, a headlamp, a first-aid kit, a fire source, repair tools, extra food, water and treatment, and emergency shelter. On a guided trek, your operator covers shelter and fire, but the rest are yours.

Winter packing list (Kedarkantha, Brahmatal, Dayara)

This list draws on the Kedarkantha and Brahmatal pages on Treqz and Himalayan Dream Treks' list for Dayara Bugyal, where winter nights can reach about -15 C.

  • Clothing: 2 to 3 thermal base sets, a fleece jacket and a sweater, a hooded down or padded jacket rated for about -10 C, a windproof and waterproof shell, 2 quick-dry trek pants, a warm hat, balaclava or neck warmer, and two pairs of gloves (a fleece liner plus a waterproof outer).
  • Feet: waterproof high-ankle shoes, 3 to 4 pairs of socks plus woollen socks for the night, gaiters and microspikes, and camp slippers.
  • Gear: a 40 to 60 litre rucksack with rain cover, a day pack, trekking poles, a head torch with spare batteries, two 1-litre bottles (one insulated so it does not freeze), UV sunglasses, and a power bank because there is no charging on the trail.
  • Papers: photo ID and a photocopy, a medical certificate and self-declaration form where the operator asks, trek insurance, and cash in small notes.
  • Health: your own medicines, blister plasters, ORS, sunscreen and lip balm, and snacks such as dry fruit.

Summer and monsoon packing list (Hampta Pass, Valley of Flowers)

Bikat's Hampta Pass list and Trekkaro's Valley of Flowers list are lighter on insulation and heavier on rain protection.

  • Rain protection: rain jacket and rain pants or a poncho, a pack rain cover with a plastic liner inside, and zip bags for phone and papers.
  • Warmth: thermal top and bottom, a fleece, and a warm jacket, since Hampta Pass nights are 0 to 5 C and Hemkund is cold in wind and mist.
  • Feet: waterproof, broken-in shoes with ankle support, gaiters for mud, and waterproof warm gloves for the pass.
  • Sun: a sun hat, sunglasses and SPF 50 sunscreen. Sun is strong at altitude, even between showers.
  • Other: a head torch, two 1-litre bottles (plastic is banned inside the Valley of Flowers), your own plate, spoon and mug on Hampta Pass, and a power bank.

Check the deposit and the condition of the gear with the shop. Many operators already give tents, sleeping bags, microspikes and gaiters, so ask what your fee covers before you rent anything. Bikat also hands out rented shoes, poles, a fleece jacket and a rucksack in Sankri for Kedarkantha.

Our suggestion: buy what touches your skin or must fit you exactly (thermals, socks, gloves, and shoes if you trek often) and rent the bulky warm layers. Our trek pages list Decathlon in Dehradun for thermals, socks, fleece, gloves, a torch and poles. The shoes and poles guide explains fit.

What should you not bring?

  • Jeans and cotton base layers. Trekkaro says to wear quick-dry trek pants and moisture-wicking layers instead.
  • Wet wipes that are not biodegradable, and single-use plastic. Govind National Park, which covers Kedarkantha and Har Ki Dun, bans polythene and non-biodegradable items and asks you to carry all garbage out.
  • Suitcases, trolleys or duffel bags if you use offloading; Trekkaro's limit is 9 kg for offloaded bags on the Valley of Flowers.
  • Alcohol. The NHS advises against drinking it while travelling or climbing at altitude.

Quick questions

What should I pack for a Himalayan trek?

Thermal base layers, a fleece, a waterproof shell, a warm jacket, quick-dry trek pants, waterproof high-ankle shoes, warm socks, gloves, a head torch, two 1-litre bottles, sunglasses, sunscreen, ID and cash. Add spikes and gaiters in winter, and rain gear in the monsoon.

Should I rent or buy trekking gear in India?

If you trek once or twice a year, renting the padded jacket, rucksack and shoes is usually cheaper. Himalayan Dream Treks lists a jacket at ₹100 to 200 a day and high-ankle shoes at ₹200 to 400 a day, depending on how many days you rent.

Can I wear jeans on a trek?

Trek operators advise against it. Trekkaro says to wear quick-dry trek pants rather than jeans, and to avoid cotton next to your skin, because cotton holds moisture and dries slowly.

What should I not bring on a trek?

Leave jeans, cotton base layers, non-biodegradable wet wipes, suitcases or duffel bags (if you offload your pack) and single-use plastic at home. Govind National Park's rules ban polythene and non-biodegradable items.
